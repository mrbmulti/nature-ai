A Lesson · Open Hypothesis

Has Nature Already Invented the Transformer?

Could evolution involve a deeper, undiscovered biological language?

Lesson 1 · The Big Idea

A question, not an answer

You will learn: what the hypothesis claims, what a transformer is, where the analogy is strong, and how a scientist would test it.

New Zealand physicist and engineer Christopher Patrick Cotton proposes that nature may have information-processing mechanisms that resemble the attention, association, feedback and inference used in modern AI. His research proposal is titled The Undiscovered Language of Life: Beyond Darwin.

He suggests genetics may hold a deeper organizational language operating beyond the genetic code as presently understood. Importantly, the hypothesis is offered as an extension of Darwinian evolution, not a replacement.

Status check: this is an open hypothesis inviting investigation, criticism and experimental testing. It is not established science, and this lesson treats it that way.
Lesson 2 · Maths in Living Things

The spiral shell

A growing shell adds material along its edge, and each turn is a scaled copy of the last. That produces a logarithmic spiral, where the radius grows by a constant factor per turn: r = a·ebθ. Simple growth rules, repeated, give precise geometry.

The hypothesis asks: if a short rule can yield a shell, could similar underlying principles help explain how nature builds the wider diversity of life?

Key idea: complex form can emerge from compact rules. This part is well established; the leap to a hidden “language” is the proposal.

Lesson 3 · The AI Side

What is a transformer?

A transformer is the neural-network design behind modern language AI. Its key trick is attention: when processing one item in a sequence, the model weighs how relevant every other item is, then blends information accordingly.

Four functions to remember: attention (what matters now?), association (what relates to what?), feedback (adjust using results), inference (derive an output from context).

Lesson 4 · Looking for the Parallel

Where might biology resemble it?

Biology already shows context-dependent control. A distant DNA switch (an enhancer) can loop across the genome to influence one gene, while many other regions are ignored. That is selective, weighted influence over distance, loosely like attention.

AI functionPossible biological counterpartStrength of evidence
AttentionEnhancers, chromatin looping, selective gene activationMechanisms real; “attention” is an analogy
AssociationProtein and gene interaction networksWell documented
FeedbackRegulatory loops, homeostasis, natural selectionWell documented
InferenceDevelopment turning genetic context into formSpeculative framing

The hypothesis goes further: that these pieces might be organised by a deeper layer of language not yet identified.

Lesson 5 · Weighing It

How would a scientist test this?

Analogy is a starting point, not proof. A strong test would state a prediction that could fail. For example: do regulatory sequences show long-range, context-weighted dependencies that current models of the genetic code do not account for? Could a model trained on genomes reveal structure beyond known codes?

Critics would ask whether the resemblance is simply because both systems must route information, and whether known biology already explains the effects. Those are the questions the proposal itself invites.

Good habits: separate what is observed, what is analogy, and what is conjecture; ask what result would change your mind.

Summary

Takeaways

• Simple rules can generate precise biological form, as in spiral shells.

• Transformers use attention, association, feedback and inference.

• Biology has real analogues of some of these; the unifying “language” is unproven.

• Cotton frames it as an extension of Darwin, open to criticism and experiment.

Dictionary Index

Glossary of terms

Associationnoun
A learned link between two items, such as words that often appear together, or genes and proteins that interact.
Attentionnoun, AI
A mechanism that weighs how relevant each part of an input is to the part currently being processed.
Chromatinnoun
DNA packaged with proteins inside the cell nucleus; its folding affects which genes can be switched on.
Darwinian evolutionnoun
Change in populations over generations through variation, inheritance and natural selection. The hypothesis is offered as an extension of it.
DNAnoun
The molecule that carries genetic instructions, written in a four-letter chemical alphabet (A, C, G, T).
Enhancernoun
A stretch of DNA that can boost the activity of a gene, sometimes from far away along the strand.
Feedbacknoun
Using the result of a process to adjust that same process, in cells, ecosystems and machine learning alike.
Genenoun
A segment of DNA that contains instructions for making a molecule such as a protein.
Genetic codenoun
The rules by which DNA triplets are translated into amino acids. The hypothesis asks whether a deeper organizational layer exists beyond it.
Homeostasisnoun
An organism’s way of holding internal conditions steady using feedback.
Hypothesisnoun
A testable proposed explanation. It is not a proven fact until evidence supports it.
Inferencenoun
Drawing a conclusion or output from available context and prior knowledge.
Logarithmic spiralnoun
A curve whose radius grows by a constant factor per turn, r = a·ebθ, seen in many shells.
Natural selectionnoun
The process by which traits that help survival and reproduction become more common over time.
Neural networknoun
A computing model of layered connected nodes whose connection strengths are learned from data.
Regulatory networknoun
The web of genes, switches and proteins that control when and how much each gene is active.
Self-attentionnoun, AI
Attention in which every element of a sequence weighs every other element of the same sequence.
Tokennoun, AI
A small unit of input, such as a word or word-piece, that a transformer processes.
Transformernoun, AI
A neural-network design built around attention, used in modern language and image AI.
